**Study Title:** Sepsis-3 definitions predict ICU mortality in a low-middle-income country  
**Authors:** Bruno Adler Maccagnan Pinheiro Besen; Thiago Gomes Romano; Antonio Paulo Nassar Jr.; Leandro Utino Taniguchi; Luciano Cesar Pontes Azevedo; Pedro Vitale Mendes; Fernando Godinho Zampieri; Marcelo Park  
**Year:** 2016  
**Journal:** Critical Care  
**Doi:** 10.1186/s13613-016-1860-0  
**Keywords:** Infection; Intensive care unit; Sepsis; Septic shock; Lactate; Organ dysfunction  
**Population:** Patients diagnosed with sepsis admitted to a 19-bed ICU in a Brazilian public university hospital  
**Sample Size:** N=957 total; N=311 died in ICU  
**Predictor:** Sepsis-3 definitions; Sepsis-2 definitions; Lactate levels  
**Outcome:** ICU mortality  
**Timing:** Measured at ICU admission  
**Method:** Retrospective cohort study; ROC analysis; logistic regression  
**Effect Size:** ICU mortality rates: Sepsis-3 (infection 7%, sepsis 25%, septic shock 46%); Sepsis-2 (severe sepsis 17%, septic shock 44%)  
**Performance:** AUC for Sepsis-3 definitions improved with lactate >4 mmol/L; NRI and IDI showed improved accuracy with lactate addition  
**Notes:** Sepsis-3 definitions were superior to Sepsis-2 in predicting ICU mortality; lactate improved accuracy only at higher levels.  
**Summary:** In a Brazilian ICU, Sepsis-3 definitions effectively stratified mortality risk, outperforming Sepsis-2 definitions, with lactate levels enhancing predictive accuracy at values above 4 mmol/L.  
**Source:** Results section, pages 5-6

**Study Title:** A risk score based on pediatric sequential organ failure assessment predicts 90-day mortality in children with Klebsiella pneumoniae bloodstream infection  
**Authors:** Shuang Li; Jingxian Liu; Feng Chen; Kang Cai; Jintong Tan; Wei Xie; Rong Qian; Xiaoqin Liu; Wenhong Zhang; Huimin Du; Ying Liu; Lisu Huang  
**Year:** 2020  
**Journal:** BMC Infectious Diseases  
**Doi:** 10.1186/s12879-020-05080-5  
**Keywords:** Klebsiella pneumoniae; Children; Mortality; Risk score  
**Population:** Pediatric patients diagnosed with Klebsiella pneumoniae bloodstream infection at Xinhua Hospital, Shanghai, China  
**Sample Size:** N=146 total; N=33 non-survivors; N=113 survivors  
**Predictor:** Pediatric Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA)  
**Outcome:** 90-day mortality  
**Timing:** At the onset of Klebsiella pneumoniae bloodstream infection  
**Method:** Retrospective cohort study; Cox proportional hazard model; ROC analysis  
**Effect Size:** AUC of SOFA = 0.80 (95% CI 0.71 - 0.89); AUC of modified SOFA model = 0.89 (95% CI 0.82 - 0.96); sensitivity = 0.86; specificity = 0.84  
**Performance:** AUC = 0.80 for SOFA; AUC = 0.89 for modified SOFA model; 90-day mortality rates: low-risk 5.4%, medium-risk 35.7%, high-risk 72.0%  
**Notes:** The modified SOFA model outperformed the original SOFA score in predicting mortality.  
**Summary:** The study found that a modified Pediatric SOFA score, combined with clinical factors, significantly improved the prediction of 90-day mortality in children with Klebsiella pneumoniae bloodstream infection.  
**Source:** Results section, pages 5-6
